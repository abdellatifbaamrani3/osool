# 03 — ICP, Market, and Buyer Psychology

Everything in this document exists to be turned into copy, section order, and objection
handling. If a page section does not map to a pain, desire, objection, or trust trigger
listed here, it should not exist.

## 1. Who she is

**Primary ICP:** Saudi woman, 24–42, urban (Riyadh, Jeddah, Dammam/Khobar) and increasingly
secondary cities. Middle to upper-middle income or a household with discretionary spend.
Smartphone-first — often smartphone-*only*. Heavy Snapchat user (Saudi Arabia has one of the
world's highest per-capita Snapchat usage rates), heavy TikTok user, uses WhatsApp for
everything including talking to businesses. Shops online constantly, and is very used to
cash on delivery. Has bought hair products before — probably several — and has been
disappointed at least twice.

**Segments inside the ICP**

| Segment | Situation | Primary product | Emotional core |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The new mother** (25–35) | 3–12 months postpartum, dramatic shedding, handfuls in the shower | Serum + Tonic | Fear that it will not stop; grief for how her hair used to look |
| **The chronically depleted** (24–40) | Constant fatigue, heavy periods, low iron, thinning at the part line | Tonic first | Exhaustion; being told "your labs are fine" |
| **The widening part** (30–45) | Gradual density loss, scalp showing under salon light, family history | Serum + Tonic | Dread of a slow, irreversible slide |
| **The irritated scalp** (22–40) | Flaking, itching, product build-up, extensions/tight styles, hair oils not washing out | Exfoliant | Embarrassment; itching in public |
| **The over-treated** (22–35) | Bleach, keratin, heat, tight styles | Exfoliant + Serum | Guilt and regret; wants a reset |

**Secondary ICP (do not design for, but do not exclude):** the same woman buying *for* her
sister, mother, or daughter. This is a real and large share of Gulf COD orders — it is one
more reason the 2- and 3-piece offers convert.

## 2. Cultural and behavioural context that changes the build

| Fact | Design consequence |
|---|---|
| Hair is deeply private but intensely discussed among women | Reviews and UGC are disproportionately persuasive. Privacy in packaging and discreet delivery must be stated explicitly. |
| Discreet delivery matters | State "يوصلك بتغليف مقفل، ما فيه أي تفاصيل عن المنتج من الخارج" near checkout. |
| WhatsApp is the default business channel | WhatsApp is the primary contact CTA everywhere, not email. Email is optional and mostly ignored. |
| COD is normal and *expected*; card entry on an unknown site is not | COD-only is an advantage, not a limitation. Sell it: "ما تدفعين ولا ريال إلا لما يوصلك". |
| Deep scepticism of new online stores (many scams) | Trust scaffolding must be dense and early: real address-level presence, CR-style transparency, named policies, a reachable human. |
| Prayer times, weekends (Fri–Sat), Ramadan rhythms | Confirmation-call windows and delivery expectations should be stated in local terms. See `30`. |
| Modesty norms | Imagery, review avatars, and video stills must be modest. Never show a woman in a way she would not share. |
| Fatigue with over-promising influencer beauty ads | Understatement converts better than hype here. Our "honest limits" copy is a *competitive* weapon. |
| Arabic-first, but code-switches naturally with English product terms | Keep "Redensyl", "Vitamin C", "Salicylic" in Latin. Do not translate ingredient names into awkward Arabic. |

## 3. Pains — in her words

Use these as literal copy seeds. They are written in the register she uses.

**Functional pains**

- «كل يوم أشوف شعري في المصفاة وفي المخدة، صرت أخاف أسرّح»
- «مفرق شعري صار أوسع، ألاحظه في الصور»
- «شعري خف من قدّام، صرت أغطيه بالطريقة اللي أسرّح بها»
- «تعبانة على طول، وحتى أظافري تتكسر»
- «فروة رأسي تحكّني وفيها قشور، وكل ما أحك يزيد التساقط»
- «جرّبت زيوت وفيتامينات وشامبوهات... ولا شي بان»

**Emotional pains** — these are what actually move the sale

- **Fear of irreversibility.** "لو ما وقفته الآن، وين بيوصل بعد سنة؟"
- **Loss of self-image.** Hair is identity. Thinning feels like watching yourself fade.
- **Feeling dismissed.** Told "it's normal", "it's postpartum", "your labs are fine".
- **Decision fatigue and self-blame.** She has already spent money on things that failed and half-believes she is the problem.
- **Anticipated embarrassment.** Someone noticing. A photo. A hairdresser commenting.

**Copy implication:** open with recognition, not with product. The first job of the hero is
to make her feel *accurately seen*. A woman who thinks "this is exactly my situation" reads
the rest of the page. A woman who reads a feature list bounces.

## 4. Desires — what she actually wants

Ranked by pull strength:

1. **For the shedding to stop.** Not growth — *stopping*. This is the number one desire and most brands under-serve it because "growth" sounds sexier. Lead with reduction of loss.
2. **Density at the part line and the temples.** The two places she checks in the mirror.
3. **To understand the cause.** Enormous unmet need. Whoever explains her situation credibly earns the sale. This is why our diagnostic framing and mechanism sections matter.
4. **A simple routine she will actually keep.** Two steps, not seven. Say the routine out loud.
5. **To feel like herself in photos again.**
6. **To not be fooled again.** Safety of decision is itself a desire. Honest limits and a guarantee serve it.

## 5. Objections — and where each one is answered

Every objection must be pre-empted *on the page*, in order of when it arises. This table is
the spine of the product page section order in `12`.

| # | Objection (as she thinks it) | Answer strategy | Page location |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | «هذا نفس أي منتج ثاني» | Named actives at stated percentages; mechanism diagram; direct comparison table vs oils/vitamins/generic serums | Mechanism + Comparison sections |
| 2 | «١٩٩ ريال غالي» | Reframe to per-day cost (≈6.6 SAR/day on the 1-piece, ≈3.9 SAR/day on the 3-piece), compare to one salon visit or one clinic session; anchor with the 3-piece | Offer block + Value reframe |
| 3 | «كيف أعرف إنه أصلي وما هو تقليد؟» | Ingredient transparency, batch/expiry visible, sealed packaging, sourcing statement | Trust strip + FAQ |
| 4 | «جرّبت أشياء كثيرة وما نفعت» | Explicitly validate it, then differentiate by *cause*: most products treat the strand or a single cause. Introduce the three-cause model. | "ليش ما نفع اللي جرّبتيه" section |
| 5 | «متى أشوف نتيجة؟» | Honest timeline with a visual: weeks 1–2 nothing visible, 4–8 less shedding, 8–12 density. Under-promise. | Timeline section |
| 6 | «هل فيه أضرار؟ أنا مرضّعة / حامل» | Safety section, tolerability, and a clear "استشيري طبيبك لو حامل أو مرضّعة" — especially for the oral tonic | Safety + FAQ |
| 7 | «ما أعرف هذا الموقع، وش يضمن لي؟» | COD (pay nothing now), named guarantee, real contact, policy pages, order volume proof | Trust strip, checkout modal, footer |
| 8 | «بيوصل؟ وكم ياخذ؟» | Delivery window per region, discreet packaging, tracking via WhatsApp | Shipping strip + FAQ |
| 9 | «أي واحد أحتاج من الثلاثة؟» | Diagnostic: cause → product mapping, and the bundle as the "لو ما تدرين، هذا الحل الكامل" answer | Home diagnostic + cross-sell |
| 10 | «أبغى أفكر» (the real closer) | Present-moment cost of delay (hair lost this month), honest scarcity, and a frictionless 2-field checkout so "thinking" never starts | Offer block + checkout |

## 6. Trust triggers, ranked for this market

Build in this order of priority — the top items are worth more than everything below them.

1. **Volume + specificity of other Saudi women's experiences.** Not "5 stars, great product" but "أسبوع ٦، التساقط قل واضح — أم ريان، الرياض". Named, located, dated, with a timeline.
2. **Real faces / real video.** UGC stills and short clips beat any badge. Reserve prominent slots for them.
3. **Cash on delivery, stated as a promise.** "تدفعين لما يوصلك" removes the single biggest transactional fear.
4. **Ingredient transparency with numbers.** Percentages and named molecules. Full INCI list available.
5. **A reachable human on WhatsApp with a stated response time.**
6. **Independent-sounding evidence.** Cited ingredient studies with honest framing (see `07`).
7. **Named, specific guarantee.** Vague "satisfaction guaranteed" is worth nothing; a named policy with a number is worth a lot.
8. **Policy completeness.** Return, shipping, privacy, terms — their mere existence, correctly written, is a trust signal.
9. **Order-count / customer-count proof.** Real numbers only, updated.
10. **Third-party badges.** Weakest of the set. Use quietly, never as the primary trust device.

**Anti-trust signals — audit the build for these**

- Stock photography a Saudi woman will recognise as western stock
- Countdown timers that reset on refresh (she *will* notice, and it poisons everything else)
- Prices ending in .99, or fake struck-through "original" prices that were never real
- Broken Arabic, mixed dialects, or visible machine translation
- LTR-leaking layout: a stray left-aligned block or a reversed icon
- No contact information, or a form that goes nowhere
- Reviews that all sound like the same person wrote them

## 7. Awareness stages and where traffic lands

Snapchat/TikTok traffic is **problem-aware, solution-unaware** — she knows her hair is
falling; she does not know about Redensyl or the three-cause model.

| Stage | Share of traffic | What the landing surface must do |
|---|---|---|
| Problem-aware, solution-unaware | ~70% | Direct product landing page. Must teach the mechanism before asking for the sale. Long page, heavy proof, mechanism + timeline are mandatory. |
| Solution-aware, brand-unaware | ~20% | Comparison and specificity sections; "why ours vs the other serum" |
| Brand-aware | ~10% | Fast path to offer; sticky ATC must always be one tap away |

This is why the product page is long *and* has a persistent sticky add-to-cart: the sceptic
gets her education, the ready buyer gets her shortcut, and neither is penalised.

## 9. Reference personas for copy and review seeding

Use these when writing sample reviews and UGC scripts. Keep seeded content clearly
placeholder in the codebase (`is_seed: true`) so real reviews can replace it.

- **نورة، ٢٩، الرياض** — 6 months postpartum, shedding in handfuls, has tried biotin gummies. Wants it to *stop*. Buys the Serum + Tonic bundle.
- **هند، ٣٦، جدة** — widening part over three years, mother had the same, sceptical, reads ingredients. Converts on the mechanism section and the honest timeline.
- **ريم، ٢٤، الدمام** — flaking and itching, uses hair oils that do not wash out, wears her hair tied tightly. Buys the Exfoliant, later adds the Serum.
- **أم فهد، ٤١، القصيم** — buying for her daughter as much as herself. Buys the 3-piece. Wants discreet delivery and to pay cash.
